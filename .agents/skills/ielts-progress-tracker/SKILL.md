# IELTS progress tracker

Use English. Check only the current conversation, explicitly provided summaries, and authorized local progress records. If no previous record is accessible, say progress is unavailable and begin a baseline. Do not invent historical sessions or silently scan unrelated personal files or Drive.

Track each error under a stable ID and skill/pattern, with the exact observed example, corrected example, brief reason, observation count, independent retrieval successes, last review, next due, and status. Call a pattern recurring after separate observations, not merely because one sentence contains several mistakes. Retrieval uses a new context before revealing the correction.

Schedule the initial retrieval for the next day. After the first, second, and third independent delayed successes, schedule retrieval in 3, 7, and 14 days respectively. Same-day retries or prompted corrections are assisted evidence and do not advance mastery. An error resets the success sequence and schedules the next day. After four delayed successes mark stabilized, retain the evidence, and spot-check after 30 days. Use the learner's local date if known; otherwise record "next session" rather than guessing a date.

Maintain a vocabulary queue with a stable ID, expression and specific meaning, collocation, source/topic, learner sentence, encounter count, recognition/production evidence, status, and next due date. Different senses may have separate entries. Review due vocabulary at every session before new items; add at most three new expressions when the review load permits. Ask for recall before showing definitions and reuse expressions in Speaking/Writing. Keep opening retrieval to two items total across vocabulary/errors (one on busy days), then integrate remaining due items into activities or carry them forward.

Use the same initial 1-day and subsequent 3/7/14-day schedule for vocabulary. Record recognition (understanding meaning) separately from production (accurate independent use in a new context). Vocabulary progresses through learning, recognized, usable, and retained. Retained requires four independent delayed successes including at least two production attempts; then review after 30 days. Recognition-only items keep an interval of at most 14 days. An incorrect retrieval resets the success sequence and schedules tomorrow. Assisted answers, copied examples, and same-day retries do not advance retention. An encounter alone does not establish recall. Retained is an observed practice status, not permanent mastery or a band. Missed days leave items overdue for the next session; do not invent practice or erase records.

Use a compact flashcard cycle during retrieval: show only the question side, wait for the learner's attempt, then reveal the meaning or example with brief feedback. Vary expression-to-meaning and context-to-expression prompts, followed by original sentence or spoken use. A definition copied from the answer side or a correct multiple-choice guess is not independent production. Accept a valid meaning explained in the learner's own words; precise target-word use and collocation still matter. Keep official Reading/Listening key rules unchanged: conceptual flexibility in vocabulary practice does not authorize fuzzy marking of test answers.

If the learner says they do not know, record the failed retrieval before revealing the answer. Offer one brief example and revisit the item later in the session in a different context; this is same-day relearning and does not replace tomorrow's delayed review. Prioritize due or recently failed items, varying order among items of equal priority. A learner's confidence label is useful context, not proof of retention. When meaning and collocation are correct but an unrelated grammar error appears, record vocabulary success and the separate grammar error rather than treating all dimensions as the same result. The helper stores the tutor/learner judgment and cannot make this semantic decision itself.

Track preferences with provenance (learner-stated or tentative inference) and revise them when corrected.

Separate tutor-verified raw marks with key/source and item IDs, learner-reported results, and inferred Writing/Speaking judgments. Record modality, timing, replays, assistance, confidence, and missing evidence. Never convert a partial set to a band. Pronunciation and a complete Speaking band remain unassessed from transcripts alone.

End each session with an exportable summary containing completed/pending activities, error queue, vocabulary, preferences, next due reviews, next Reading/Writing focus, and one next action. Use `templates/session-log.md` and `templates/weekly-report.md` if present. Displaying a summary is not saving it. State a storage location only after a successful authorized write.

Keep real learner records in ignored `private/` or an explicitly chosen private destination, never GitHub commits or test fixtures. The optional `tools/coach.py` helper stores error and vocabulary queues; it does not replace the full summary or assess language automatically. Use `vocabulary-due`, `add-vocabulary`, and `review-vocabulary` for local vocabulary records when writes are authorized; record tutor/learner judgments with actual response evidence. Without local tools, use the same fields in the exported summary. Do not access or modify Drive without separate authorization. Repository examples must be synthetic and contain no real learner history or credentials.
